2. What Linezy does

Linezy reads your shop receipts (photos and PDFs) into lists of items and prices, so you can see what you spend and how prices change. You can also type receipts in by hand. You can keep warranties with your receipts and get reminders on your phone, export your receipts, and share them with your household.

Linezy works on iPhones with iOS 15 or later and on Android phones; the store listing shows the Android versions it supports. Signing in, reading receipts and syncing need an internet connection; you can look at your receipts and make changes without one, and they are sent when you're back online.

Linezy does not handle money. It does not link to bank accounts, make payments, or move money between people. It has no ads.

5. Reading receipts with AI

  • When you scan, import or share a receipt into Linezy, it is sent to Google Gemini, an AI service, which reads it. This happens only after your explicit consent on the welcome screen. If you say no, you can still type receipts in by hand; typed receipts count in your spending but don't build price history. You can switch AI reading on or off at any time in Settings → Privacy and data; switching it off keeps the receipts you already have.
  • AI makes mistakes. Shops, dates, totals, items, prices, discounts and categories can be read wrongly. Mistakes are more likely when a receipt is faded, creased or torn, when the photo is blurred, dark or cut off, or when the print is small or unusual. Linezy shows what it is unsure about ("Check this") and lets you correct everything. Check the figures before you rely on them, especially before you settle money with someone else.
  • Some receipts can't be read at all. A receipt that can't be read does not count against your free scans or your daily scans, and it stays saved so you can try again.

9. Splitting expenses

If a member of a household switches it on, Linezy works out who owes whom from the household's shared receipts, the split the household sets (for example 60/40), who paid (or that it was paid from joint money, which counts in spending but not in who owes whom), and items marked as one person's. Splitting counts only receipts from the day it is switched on, bought while both the person who added the receipt and the payer were in the household. Any member can change the split and who paid, and everyone can see who made a change. When someone joins, the split goes back to equal; when someone leaves, their share is divided among the others in proportion. Any member can set it again.

A private receipt, even one private only until a date, doesn't count in the split, also after that date. If you make it shared again, it counts from then on. Showing the balance, marking a month settled and the split's Excel file will need a Household plan. The Household plan is not available yet; we will tell you by e-mail if it becomes available, together with its price.

Linezy only calculates. It does not collect or pay anything, and it is not a payment service, financial advice or a legal record of what people owe each other. The result is only as right as the receipts and settings behind it.
